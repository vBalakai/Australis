# Australis Region Design Bible

## Overview

Australis is an Australia-inspired region where the journey is a road trip clockwise around the country, ending at a wildlife sanctuary just north of where it began.

The core theme is trust over ownership. The villains are poachers protected by a corrupt government; the heroes are the people who heal and protect Pokémon. The strongest trainer in the region is a vet, not a battler.

Working titles for the paired versions: *Pokémon Ember* and *Pokémon Monsoon*.

## Map

The player starts in Wattle Town, loops clockwise through eight gyms, crosses to Tasmania, and finishes at Glasshouse Sanctuary near the start. Travel between towns is by plane only: every town has an airport, each badge unlocks the next flight, and flights taken become fast travel. Local routes branch out from each airport, and bush charters reach Opal Hollow and The Endless Road.

**Routes and special areas**

- **Route 1** (Darling Downs): red dirt farm roads; wheat and sorghum crops are the tall grass
- **Bloomridge** (Toowoomba): pass-through garden town on the range, no gym; healing centre, store, flower park, lookout and the first Team Snare encounter
- **Route 2** (Lockyer Valley): stairs down the escarpment into green valley farms; corn is the tall grass
- **Surf Coast Route** (Gold Coast): beaches and water routes
- **Canopy Forest** (Daintree): ancient rainforest dungeon, Bug and Grass types
- **Reef Depths** (Great Barrier Reef): diving area, rare Water types
- **Opal Hollow** (Coober Pedy): underground dugout town, rare stones
- **The Endless Road** (Nullarbor Plain): long straight cliff route with whales offshore
- **The Super Pit** (Kalgoorlie): open-cut mine dungeon, Steel types, Team Snare smuggling camp
- **Twelve Pillars Coast** (Great Ocean Road): sea stacks, legendary in the caves
- **Harbour Metropolis** (Sydney): biggest city, no gym, Team Snare HQ
- **Blue Haze Mountains** (Blue Mountains): misty cliffs, Ghost and Psychic types
- **Old Gaol Ruins** (Port Arthur): haunted prison, Ghost types
- **Victory Road**: through the Glass House Mountains to the sanctuary
- **Capital Hill** (Canberra): seat of the corrupt Ministry, post-game

The Red Centre uses a fictional giant rock instead of Uluru, which is sacred to the Anangu people.

## Story and villains

Team Snare poaches rare Australis Pokémon, and the Ministry of Wildlife Trade in Capital Hill secretly protects them and trades the Pokémon overseas.

**Team Snare** smuggles native Pokémon to foreign collectors. Its boss is the public face, running a respectable export company in Harbour Metropolis. Admins run field operations, such as the Super Pit camp and the Harbour Metropolis docks.

**The Ministry of Wildlife Trade** officially protects wildlife and approves exports. In secret, its permits make stolen Pokémon look legal, and investigations into Team Snare get buried. In return for native Pokémon, foreign regions send their regional forms, which only the political elite may own.

**The Inner Circle** is a handful of senators whose teams are built on these foreign forms. **The Minister** is the real mastermind and wants the serpent legendary, the most valuable trade in history.

**Plot beats**

1. Early foreshadowing: a politician on TV with a Pokémon never seen in the wild; grunts complain that "the suits get all the good ones."
2. Rescues: the player frees stolen Pokémon from Team Snare across the journey.
3. Mid-game crisis: Team Snare raids an ancient site and wakes the two box legendaries, causing bushfires and floods at once.
4. The serpent rises and calms them. The Ministry publicly calls it a myth and mass hysteria.
5. Climax: Team Snare storms Glasshouse Sanctuary to break into the Heartland. The player and the Elite Four defend it.
6. The Heartland: the player finds the serpent wounded, wearing a Ministry tracking tag registered years ago. This proves the government knew it was real. The serpent chooses the player.
7. The player becomes Champion; the Team Snare boss is arrested and leaves evidence pointing to Capital Hill.

**Post-game**

The player goes to Capital Hill, exposes the Ministry and battles the Inner Circle, then the Minister. The ending is about mercy: the Champion helps rehabilitate the foreign Pokémon and return them home, and the stolen natives come back to Australis.

## Legendaries

Two box legendaries represent the north's wet and dry seasons, and a serpent legendary keeps them in balance.

| Legendary | Type | Concept |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drouthorn | Fire/Ground | Cracked-earth beast that smoulders like burnt eucalyptus; fire as both destruction and regrowth. Cover of *Ember*. |
| Monsurge | Water/Electric | Storm creature of the northern coast that brings the rains and cyclones. Cover of *Monsoon*. |
| Prismasp (working name) | Dragon/Fairy | Lets the rains follow the drought. Reached in the Heartland using scales from four guardian serpents; see the [Pokédex](pokedex.md). |

**Cultural note:** the Rainbow Serpent is a sacred ancestral being for many First Nations peoples. The recommended approach is an original serpent inspired by the idea, with its own name and lore, rather than the sacred figure itself.

## Starters

Each starter's second type is super effective against the type it is weak to, a fuller version of the Gen 4 pattern.

| Line | Final type | Second type hits | Signature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gumjo → Bellowala → Koalossus (koala) | Grass/Ground | Fire | Gum Bellow: sound attack that can cause flinching |
| Kindrill → Frillblaze → Pyragon (frilled lizard) | Fire/Electric | Water | Frill Flare: lowers the foe's Attack on entry |
| Puggly → Spurbill → Platyrant (platypus) | Water/Poison | Grass | Electrosense: its moves never miss |

- **Koalossus:** a bush guardian with a eucalyptus grove on its back, rooted deep into the earth. Deliberately loud and awake, unlike the existing sleepy koala Pokémon, Komala.
- **Pyragon:** its frill crackles with static, a nod to the dry lightning that starts outback bushfires. Frill-necked lizards are "dragon lizards," hence the name.
- **Platyrant:** a creek guardian with a venomous ankle spur. "Puggle" is the real word for a baby platypus.

Balance check: Pyragon's Electric does nothing to Koalossus, and Koalossus's Ground also hits Platyrant hard. Platyrant may need an Ice coverage move.

## Gym leaders

Levels rise about 5–6 per gym and teams grow from 2 to 6 Pokémon, while strategy grows from one simple trick to a full weather team.

| 2 | Marlee, reef dive instructor | Coralport | Water | 19–21 | 3 | Status moves and bulk |
| 3 | Ray, storm chaser | Stormhaven | Electric | 25–27 | 3 | Paralysis and speed |
| 4 | Ranger Dusty, outback ranger | Redheart | Ground | 31–33 | 4 | Weather: sandstorm |
| 5 | Gale, kitesurfer | Breezeport | Flying | 36–38 | 4 | Speed control with Tailwind; ace holds an item |
| 6 | Harmony, choir director | Vinegrove | Fairy | 41–43 | 5 | Terrain: Misty Terrain, stat drops |
| 7 | Coach Mack, retired sports star | Laneway City | Fighting | 46–48 | 5 | Setup moves and switching |
| 8 | Dr. Frost, Antarctic researcher | Frostport | Ice | 51–54 | 6 | Full weather team: snow and Aurora Veil |

**Team-building rules**

- Story teams mix regional-dex Pokémon with the leader's native ace, as a real regional dex would.
- From Gym 5, every ace holds an item; from Gym 7, the whole team does.
- Each team has a plan: a lead that sets up the field, support, then the ace last.
- No leader uses a foreign regional form, because in the lore only the political elite can own them.
- Story arc: Ranger Dusty is the first adult to suspect the Ministry and helps the player in the post-game.
